Which payment provider handles an order

The vendor's country picks the provider, not the payer's. A relative in Dublin paying a Lagos mortuary pays through Paystack, which accepts international cards. In the code, both providers sit behind one PaymentGateway interface, and every movement of money is recorded as a pair of entries in an internal ledger.

Security built into the setup

  • Postgres and the private bucket are unreachable from the internet. SSH is open only to Forge's IP addresses.
  • Two-factor sign-in is required for vendors and all staff roles. Staff sessions time out sooner, and refunds, payouts and bank-detail changes ask for the password again.
  • Changing a bank account sends an email and SMS alert and holds payouts for 48 hours. Large refunds and payouts need a second admin to approve them.
  • The ledger and audit-log tables are append-only: database triggers block updates and deletes. Every document view is logged.
  • Bank details and ID numbers are encrypted in their database columns. Uploaded photos have their GPS data removed.
  • A Policy on every model, and vendors can only see their own records. Private data never goes into Inertia props, because anything in props is visible in the page source.
  • Content Security Policy and HSTS headers. Rate limits on sign-in, sign-in codes, checkout and donations.
  • An external penetration test before Phase 1 goes live.

Milestones

Everything in the checklist is in scope. The three main products come first: the local marketplace, crowdfunding, then savings. Each milestone ends with a release to production and sign-off. The checklist IDs show where each of the 174 features lands. Weeks assume one full-time developer, with a designer for the first four weeks.
